# Mallside v5: ten destination research

Research date: 2026-10-25. Primary sources below establish architectural cues, amenities and broad retailer mix. The proposed sequence is a game design interpretation, not an objective ranking of real malls. Each destination should use original Mallside geometry and art, with an “inspired by” description. The curated real-product catalog and simulated prices do not represent live inventory, exact tenant placement or an affiliation with these centers.

## Progression and navigation guidance

All ten real centers contain multiple price points. An XP gate should describe the player's game progress, not imply that every product in one real mall is more expensive or better than every product in another. Desert Hills supplies a useful designer-outlet step before full-price luxury-oriented destinations. Keep accessible cafes, rest areas and lower-cost browsing opportunities throughout the career.

Award progression for completed missions, discoveries, comparisons, outfit creation and staying within a plan, alongside purchases. Higher destinations should add landmarks, route choices and activities as well as higher-priced items. Preserve prior destinations for unfinished requests, favorites and returns.

Use connected walkable sections within each original destination: entrance to shopping lane, lane to court, court to dining or garden, and a clearly marked route onward. Crossing a boundary should preserve facing, destination and current needs, show the next section's name, and place the player on a walkable tile. Keep travel between separate malls in a clearly presented destination selector; do not suggest the geographically separate real malls are one contiguous physical complex.

Each destination needs a recognizable landmark, a functional food or rest stop, visible onward connections and a useful social interaction. A quiet pond pause, cafe purchase, seated bench rest, concierge visit and pop-up browse provide variety without requiring new invented products.

## Character and presentation implications

Bought hats, bags, belts, shoes and clothing should produce persistent visible equipment. Equip only owned, eligible items; remove or replace equipment when its underlying item is returned or delivered. Give the wardrobe readable categories, a preview, item names and an unequip control.

Show walking, seated rest, eating, drinking and sleep as distinct states. Animation duration should never determine economic rewards: a cancelled or skipped presentation must not duplicate spending, recovery, XP or day advancement. Reduced-motion settings can shorten or simplify the same interactions while preserving their result.

The menu should clearly expose Continue, New Career, How to Play, destinations, wardrobe and settings. On phone and tablet, keep touch controls and primary actions reachable without covering the avatar or objectives. Test narrow and wide viewports, keyboard and touch, and distinguish browser/device emulation from testing on physical hardware.
