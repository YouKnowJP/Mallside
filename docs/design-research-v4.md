# Mallside v4: research and playable design brief

Research date: 2026-10-24. This is a design interpretation of primary sources, not a claim that the game recreates South Coast Plaza or its current inventory. Keep the Mallside name, original layout, original art, and simulated economy.

## What to borrow from a real shopping destination

South Coast Plaza’s official directory separates categories and multiple levels, includes both map and list views, and marks practical amenities. That suggests an overview for orientation plus readable district maps and a searchable directory, with the same locations reachable through either route. [Official directory](https://www.southcoastplaza.com/directory%20?view=map)

Its Bridge of Gardens connects the two sides across Bear Street. Its architecture gallery documents the Garden Terrace, Jewel Court dome, and architectural details. Translate those spatial ideas into an original glass-roof court, planted bridge, distinct promenade wings, and a garden terrace, using color, silhouettes, planting, water, and signs to make districts recognizable. Do not copy an exact floor plan or present Ross/Marshalls as South Coast Plaza tenants. [Bridge of Gardens](https://www.southcoastplaza.com/directory/bridge-of-garden/), [architecture gallery](https://www.southcoastplaza.com/theedit/2022/03/the-gallery/)

The center describes two carousels, four concierge locations, a stylist program, package check, and dining that ranges from quick bites to fine dining. These can become game activities with decisions, time costs, and persistent outcomes rather than scenery alone. [Carousels](https://www.southcoastplaza.com/concierge/carousel-locations/), [concierge](https://www.southcoastplaza.com/concierge), [dining](https://www.southcoastplaza.com/dining/)

## Complete repeatable loop

**Choose a shopping plan at home → travel to a district → meet a request giver → browse and compare actual models → fit/demo → buy or decline → take a meal/rest/activity break → finish the request → walk to the exit → walk home → recap and sleep → wake to a changed day.**

The design recommendations below are inferences, informed by the cited sources. They are not descriptions of already implemented Mallside features.

1. **Make route choice meaningful.** Six visually distinct wings, connected by a planted bridge and central court. Clearly label the current district and destination. The overview should allow one tap to enter a wing; storefront buttons should walk the avatar to a door before opening a shop. Show a destination marker and allow movement cancellation.
2. **Add daily shopping briefs with exact criteria.** Examples: find a bottle under a stated tax-inclusive budget; compare one identical brand/model at two shops; find a work top in the shopper’s size; pick a real LEGO set for a gift. Give a small candidate shortlist or objective progress so requests are solvable. Reward successful delivery once, and prevent selling or returning a delivered item from duplicating payouts. Nintendo’s official Styling Star page emphasizes listening to customers and choosing an outfit for their situation; use this structure without copying its characters or dialog. [Nintendo](https://www.nintendo.com/au/games/nintendo-3ds/new-style-boutique-3-styling-star/)
3. **Give the week a rhythm.** Rotate a finite, disclosed calendar: garden showcase, sneaker demo, styling day, gift hunt, food tasting, carousel afternoon. Put the calendar on the menu and at concierge. Activities should change an objective, stamp, relationship, or reward, with once-per-day claims. The official Stardew description supports skill growth, scheduled characters, festivals, customization, and varied activities as a long-term life-sim structure. [Stardew Valley](https://www.stardewvalley.net/about/)
4. **Let the player express a persistent identity.** Main menu with Continue Career, Free Shop, How to Play, Options, and a reviewed New Career action. A profile can keep name, skin/hair/clothing colors, movement/animation preferences, clock speed, and reduced motion. Use actual owned clothing or accessories for outfit selection; do not fabricate product identities. Fashion Dreamer’s official site describes avatar styling, selfies, and showrooms; personal expression can reward exploration without replacing the shopping economy. [Fashion Dreamer](https://fashion-dreamer.com/)
5. **Treat a shopping plan as a player goal.** Let players pin several real product IDs to a list, choose a spending target, and see paid total including tax against the plan. Keep wishlist distinct from a checkout bag. Budgeting and comparison shopping support a deliberate decision loop instead of rewarding spending alone. [FDIC budgeting and shopping](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/chapter-3-budgeting-and-shopping), [CFPB buying-plan guide](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_building_block_activities_using-buying-plan_guide.pdf)
6. **Make services useful.** Concierge can provide the directory, calendar, active request, package check, and a return-deadline reminder. Package check should release a genuine carrying constraint and preserve ownership; retrieval must be explicit or automatically included in the end-of-day route so the player cannot lose purchases. A carousel ride or garden pause should cost time, produce a stamp or small recovery, and offer a skip/reduced-motion path.
7. **Finish the day visibly.** At closing, finish a safe active transition, stop shopping, and offer the home sequence. The avatar should walk from its actual location to an exit, then across an original evening street to a home door and bed. Pause the game clock during this presentation. Only the final sleep action advances the day and pays income, exactly once. Make the sequence skippable, respect reduced motion, and show a morning recap with goals, spending, needs, and tomorrow’s event.
8. **Make more stock produce more choices.** Group hundreds of actual models by retailer/category and search by normalized name/brand; load cards in chunks and show listing counts. Mark illustrations, official sources, generation/model references, unavailable sizes, and simulated prices consistently. Test or compare matching brand plus model, not just a similar title. Distinguish clothing fit from tech/beauty/hobby demos and from opening a purchased sealed item.

## Shopping realism without misleading policy claims

Ross’s official policy supports unused/unworn/unaltered merchandise with a receipt for a refund within thirty days; older/no-receipt cases differ, and exceptions apply. Marshalls similarly describes thirty-day receipt refunds, condition restrictions, merchandise-credit cases, and returns within the Marshalls brand. These support itemized receipts, condition, a deadline, and retailer-specific return desks as mechanics. They do not justify claiming every retailer uses one uniform rule. Keep a visible “game return policy” label; do not request real identity or payment details. [Ross](https://www.rossstores.com/contact-us/), [Marshalls](https://www.marshalls.com/us/store/jump/topic/easy-returns/18400006p)

The FTC recommends checking product details, comparing the full price, and reading return terms before a purchase. In Mallside, show model identity, price plus tax, selected size, final-sale status, and the relevant receipt condition before confirmation. Keep refunds tied to the original paid amount even after a coupon or daily price changes. [FTC shopping guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping)

## Verification priorities

- Unit/economy: integer cents and tax, stock conservation, atomic failed actions, exact paid-price refunds, coupon consumption/expiry, used-item restrictions, repeated reward claims, request matching and delivery ownership, event rollover, save migration, and malformed imports.
- Movement: keyboard, click-to-walk, touch direction, diagonal normalization, blocked shop footprints, idle vs moving animation, interrupted paths, open-menu input suppression, route completion, home walk/sleep exactly once, and reduced-motion skip.
- Live flows: first-career tutorial through purchase/return; career locked boutique browsing; free-shop checkout; request completion and attempted duplicate reward; coffee/rest; calendar/event claim; full end-of-day sequence and next-day resume; save/reload; main-menu options; back navigation and modal focus.
- Viewports: narrow phone, tablet portrait/landscape, desktop. Check no page overflow, visible navigation, readable objectives, full-width menus, close buttons, safe-area bottom padding, and product image fallback. Verify Chrome and Safari. Do not claim physical iPhone/iPad hardware testing from viewport emulation alone.
